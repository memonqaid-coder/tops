Q:Question: Which development methodology would you recommend for this project, and why?
  Identify two specific risks the team would face if they chose the wrong methodology for this
  context.

A:Recommended Methodology: Agile
  For a project with frequently changing requirements and a bi-weekly feature review cycle, Agile (specifically Scrum) is the highly 
  recommended development methodology.

Why Agile?
 >Adaptability to Change: Agile is designed to embrace evolving specifications. Instead of fixing scope early, it allows the team to 
  adapt and reprioritize the backlog at the end of every iteration based on client feedback.

 >Iterative Delivery: The two-week review cycle perfectly aligns with Agile Sprints (typically 1–4 week timeboxes). At the end of each
  sprint, a potentially shippable increment of the product is delivered, matching the client's expectations for a working feature review.

Risks of Choosing the Wrong Methodology (Waterfall):
 >If the team incorrectly chooses Waterfall in this context, they would face the following significant risks:

Scope Creep and Budget Overruns: Waterfall requires extensive upfront planning and rigid documentation. Because the requirements are 
  fluid, changing them midway requires an expensive and time-consuming "Change Request" process, leading to severe project delays and blown 
  budgets.

Client Dissatisfaction and Misalignment: Waterfall typically presents a working product only at the very end of the lifecycle. Forcing a 
  bi-weekly review in a Waterfall setup is structurally impossible, as development and testing happen late. The client would not see working 
  features every two weeks, leading to a breakdown in trust and a high risk that the final product won't meet their actual needs.
